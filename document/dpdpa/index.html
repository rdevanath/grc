    IND Compliance

    Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and Rules, 2025 (Consolidated)

    1. Scope & Applicability

    • Territorial
      Applies to processing in India and outside India if linked to goods/services offered to Indian individuals.
    • Material
      Covers digital personal data and digitized offline data.
    • Exemptions
      Domestic/personal use, publicly available data, certain government functions (security, sovereignty, public order), and research/statistical purposes (per Rules 2025).

    2. Stakeholders

    • Data Principals: Individuals whose data is processed.
    • Data Fiduciaries: Entities deciding purpose/means of processing.
    • Significant Data Fiduciaries (SDFs): Large-scale processors with extra obligations (DPO, audits, DPIA).
    • Data Processors: Process data on behalf of fiduciaries.
    • Consent Managers: Registered intermediaries to manage consent flows.
    • Data Protection Board of India: Regulator enforcing compliance, penalties, grievance redressal.

    3. Grounds of Processing

    • Consent: Free, specific, informed, unconditional, unambiguous, affirmative. Withdrawable anytime.
    • Legitimate Uses: Legal obligations, medical emergencies, employment, public interest, disaster response, etc.

    4. Rights of Data Principals

    • Access, correction, erasure (where consent is the ground).
    • Withdraw consent.
    • Grievance redressal (response within 90 days under Rules 2025).
    • Nomination rights (assign another person to exercise rights in case of death/incapacity).

    5. Obligations of Data Fiduciaries

    • Provide clear privacy notices (English or 22 Indian languages).
    • Implement safeguards (encryption, masking, monitoring, backups).
    • Establish grievance redressal mechanisms.
    • Delete data once purpose is fulfilled or consent withdrawn.
    • Notify breaches to Board and affected individuals.
    • Maintain contracts with Data Processors.

    Additional for SDFs

    • Appoint DPO in India and independent data auditor.
    • Conduct annual audits & DPIAs.
    • Ensure algorithmic transparency.

    6. Consent Managers

    • Must register with the Data Protection Board.
    • Provide interoperable digital platforms for consent management.
    • Retain consent records for 7 years.
    • Cannot subcontract obligations or have conflicts of interest.
    • Subject to independent certification and audits.

    7. Children’s Data

    • Requires verifiable parental consent.
    • Prohibits tracking, behavioral monitoring, targeted advertising.
    • Exemptions: Schools, healthcare professionals, crèches, child transport providers.

    8. Cross-Border Data Transfer

    • Allowed unless restricted by the Central Government (blacklist approach).
    • SDFs must comply with restrictions on sensitive categories identified by government.

    9. Breach Notification (Rules 2025)

    • Primary intimation to Board: Without delay upon becoming aware.
    • Secondary intimation to Board: Within 72 hours with detailed information.
    • Notification to Data Principals: Without delay, including nature, impact, mitigation measures, and contact details.

    10. Record Retention (Rules 2025)

    • General Rule: Retain logs of processing transactions for at least 1 year.
    • Special Classes (E-commerce, Gaming, Social Media):
      • Data Fiduciaries in the following classes must retain personal data for only three years from the last time the Data Principal approached them:
        • E-commerce entities with at least 2 crore registered users in India
        • Online gaming intermediaries with at least 50 lakh registered users in India
        • Social media intermediaries with at least 2 crore registered users in India
      • Must notify Data Principals 48 hours before deletion.
    • Consent Managers: Retain consent records for 7 years.

    11. Exemptions from Retention

    • Certain classes of fiduciaries (e.g., healthcare providers, educational institutions, child transport providers) are exempt from strict parental consent obligations when processing children’s data.
    • Research, archiving, and statistical processing exempt if lawful, necessary, accurate, and safeguarded.

    12. Penalties

    • Data Fiduciaries: Up to ₹250 crore.
    • Breach of security safeguards: Up to ₹250 crore.
    • Breach of children’s data obligations: Up to ₹200 crore.
    • Breach of SDF obligations: Up to ₹150 crore.
    • Data Principals: Fine up to ₹10,000 for misuse of rights/frivolous complaints.
    • Consent Managers: Penalties for non-compliance.

    13. Compliance Journey

    1. Design privacy policies & procedures.
    2. Serve privacy notices at data collection points.
    3. Document legal grounds of processing.
    4. Obtain and manage consent (via Consent Manager if needed).
    5. Implement safeguards (encryption, monitoring, backups).
    6. Enable rights requests & grievance redressal.
    7. Maintain breach notification templates.
    8. Review Data Processor contracts.
    9. Erase personal data after purpose expiry.
    10. For SDFs: Appoint DPO, conduct DPIAs, audits.

    DPDP Compliance Journey

    Nov 2025 – May 2026 (0–6 months after notification)

    • Develop/refresh data privacy framework.
    • Draft and publish privacy notices (categories of data, purposes, withdrawal mechanisms, grievance process).
    • Conduct data mapping (repositories, flows, recipients).
    • Implement baseline security safeguards (encryption, access controls, log monitoring, backups, DR/BCP).
    • Update contracts with third parties to include privacy obligations.
    • Establish breach response procedures (notify Board + Data Principals without undue delay).
    • Launch data privacy training programs.
    • Define data retention procedures (logs retained ≥1 year, 3 year retention schedule for special classes).

    May – Nov 2026 (6–12 months)

    • Establish mechanisms for:
      • Annual Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIAs).
      • Independent annual audits.
      • Algorithmic transparency & fairness assessments.
    • Prepare for potential data localisation requirements.
    • Implement Privacy Enabling Technologies (PETs) for automation.
    • Undertake external certifications (e.g., Privacy Information Management System – PIMS).

    Nov 2026 – May 2027 (Beyond 12–18 months)

    Full compliance with remaining Rules:

    • Consent Manager registration & obligations (Rule 4).
    • Children’s data obligations (verifiable parental consent, exemptions for schools/healthcare).
    • Processing of data for persons with disabilities (guardian consent verification).
    • Additional obligations for Significant Data Fiduciaries (SDFs) (DPO, audits, DPIAs).
    • Cross border transfer compliance (Rule 15).
    • Research/statistical exemptions (Rule 16).
    • Rights of Data Principals (Rule 14).
    • Appeals to Appellate Tribunal (Rule 22).
