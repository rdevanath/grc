    Compliance

    European Union - Artificial Intelligence Act, 2024

    1. Scope

    • Applies to AI systems placed on the EU market or used in the EU, regardless of origin.
    • Covers both public and private sector use.
    • Excludes personal/non‑professional use.

    2. Key Definitions

    • AI System - Software using ML, logic‑based, or statistical methods that generate outputs influencing environments.
    • Providers - Entities developing or placing AI systems on the market.
    • Deployers - Individuals or organizations deploying AI systems.

    3. Risk Categories

    The Act introduces a risk‑based framework:

    1. Unacceptable Risk - Prohibited AI systems

    • Manipulation of human behaviour, opinions, and decisions

    • Classification of people based on their social behaviour

    • Real-time remote biometric identification (except for limited exceptions)

    2. High Risk - Permitted subject to compliance with AI requirements

    • AI system applications listed in Annex III

    • AI systems in safety components already subject to a harmonized EU standard (Annex I)

    3. Limited Risk - Permitted but subject to transparency obligations

    Examples: chatbots, deepfakes (must disclose they’re AI).

    4. Minimal Risk - Permitted without restrictions

    Examples: spam filters, video games.

    4. Prohibited AI systems

    Use cases include:

    • Social scoring by governments

    • Manipulative AI exploiting vulnerabilities (children, disabled)

    • Biometric categorization based on sensitive attributes

    • Real‑time biometric identification in public spaces (exceptions for law enforcement)

    • Predictive policing based solely on profiling

    • Emotion recognition in workplaces and education

    5. High‑Risk Applications

    High‑risk AI systems are those that impact safety or fundamental rights.

    Annex III (Applications)

    Any application falling within Annex III categories that potentially threaten health, safety, fundamental rights, the environment, democracy, or the rule of law is automatically considered High risk.

    • Biometric identification & categorization

    • Critical infrastructure (transport, energy)

    • Education & vocational training

    • Employment & worker management

    • Access to essential services (credit scoring, social benefits)

    • Law enforcement

    • Migration, asylum & border control

    • Administration of justice & democratic processes

    Obligations

    • Providers (developers/vendors) of high risk AI systems must carry out the conformity assessment before placing the system on the market or putting it into service

    • Deployers must conduct Fundamental Rights Impact Assessments (FRIA) before use

    • Effective from Q3 2026

    Annex I (Safety Components)

    Annex I covers AI systems that are safety components of products already regulated under existing EU product safety laws.

    • Civil aviation, vehicles, marine equipment, medical devices, rail systems, personal protection, etc.,

    • These AI components must comply with the existing sectoral legislation (like the Medical Devices Regulation, Machinery Regulation, etc.).

    • Conformity assessment obligations for Annex I systems are aligned with those sectoral laws, not the standalone AI Act process - meaning they are enforceable once the AI Act enters into force (before August 2027) and the sectoral rules apply.

    • Oversight handled by an independent, accredited notified bodies

    Conformity Assessment

    Two pathways:

    • Internal Control (Self‑Assessment)

      • Applies to Annex III high risk applications (e.g., employment, essential services)

      • Providers may conduct self assessment if harmonized EU standards exist

      • If harmonized standards are missing, providers must follow common specifications (issued by the EU Commission)

      • Responsibility lies entirely with the provider to ensure compliance, maintain documentation, and issue a declaration of conformity

    • Third‑Party Assessment

      • Applies to Annex I systems (AI safety components in regulated products like aviation, medical devices, automotive)

      • Always required when harmonized standards are missing or incomplete

      • Also required for particularly sensitive high risk applications, such as biometric identification systems

      • Conducted by an independent, accredited notified body, ensuring impartiality and credibility.

    Key compliance elements:

    • Risk management system

    • Data governance

    • Technical documentation

    • Logging & record‑keeping

    • Transparency to users

    • Human oversight

    • Accuracy, robustness, cybersecurity

    Harmonized Standards

    • They are technical standards developed by European standardization bodies (like CEN, CENELEC, ETSI)

    • Provide technical guidance on how to comply with the essential requirements of EU legislation

    • Internal conformity assessment is sufficient when harmonized standards exist and the provider follows them

    Example: Employment AI (recruitment tools)

    • An AI system screens CVs and ranks candidates

    • If harmonized standards exist for fairness and non discrimination in employment, the provider can self assess compliance by applying those standards

    • If no harmonized standard exists yet, the provider must undergo a third party assessment by a notified body to ensure impartial verification

    Fundamental Rights Impact Assessment (FRIA)

    Steps:

    1. Define AI system & context

    2. Identify stakeholders & affected groups

    3. Assess impacts on fundamental rights (privacy, nondiscrimination, freedom of expression, access to justice)

    4. Evaluate severity & likelihood

    5. Document mitigation measures

    6. Establish oversight & accountability

    7. Ensure transparency & reporting

    8. Update regularly before deployment or when risks change

    6. General Purpose AI (GPAI) Models

    As models and systems are treated separately, a general-purpose AI model itself will not be classified as a high-risk AI system. However, High impact GPAI models (those with significant capabilities or risks) have stricter obligation requirements

    • Providers of GPAI models must document training data sources, evaluate systemic risks, and share technical information with downstream deployers.
    • High‑impact GPAI models must undergo risk assessments, adversarial testing, cybersecurity

    Open‑source GPAI models are not considered high-risk, however they have to provide detailed summary about the content used for training and abide with EU copyright laws

    7. Incident Reporting

    • Serious incidents - Report within 15 days
    • Critical infrastructure disruption - Report immediately, within a maximum of 2 days

    8. Sandbox Testing Facilities

    • National authorities provide regulatory sandboxes
    • Controlled testing with regulator feedback
    • Especially useful for SMEs and startups

    9. Enforcement & Penalties

    • Supervised by national authorities and coordinated by the European AI Board
    • Penalties:
      • Up to €35M or 7% of global turnover for prohibited practices
      • Up to €15M or 3% of turnover for high‑risk/GPAI infringements
      • Up to €7.5M or 1% of turnover for misleading information

    10. Implementation Timeline

    • Aug 2024 - Act enters into force
    • Q1 2025 - Prohibited practices are enforceable
    • Q3 2025 - Obligations for GPAI models and governance structures (European AI Office, national authorities) are applicable.
    • Q3 2026 - High‑risk AI system requirements under Annex III (conformity assessments, FRIA, provider obligations) become fully enforceable.
    • Q3 2027 - Full enforcement of the Act

    Exceptions:

    • High Risk AI Systems already on the market are not subject to the AI Act, if they were placed on the market before the Act entered into force

      Exception: If the system undergoes a significant change (e.g., retraining, new functionality, expanded scope), then it must comply with the Act

    • GPAI obligations start Aug 2026

    • Large‑scale EU IT systems in Freedom, Security & Justiec (e.g., Schengen), their compliance timeline is extended until 2030
